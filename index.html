A sovereign, GDPR-compliant tool assembly for clinical and translational research data

Architecture and use in European multi-centre studies

Kavita Rege, David Fitala, Sandrine Medves, Soumyabrata Ghosh* & Venkata Satagopam

Luxembourg Centre for Systems Biomedicine, University of Luxembourg
*soumyabrata.ghosh@uni.lu

Multi-centre European health studies increasingly rely on collecting, harmonising and sharing sensitive data across countries with different legal frameworks, technical capacities and clinical workflows. A single monolithic platform rarely fits all of these needs, while hyperscaler cloud solutions raise concerns about data sovereignty and GDPR compliance. We present the clinical and translational research data infrastructure developed at the Luxembourg Centre for Systems Biomedicine: not one system, but a governed assembly of interoperable tools and services.

Efficient tool assembly

We combine open-source tools, academically licensed software, and commercial services where they add value with in-house developments: web and mobile applications, custom plugins, API handlers, ETL and curation pipelines, and Adept analytics platform. Core services run on university-hosted infrastructure.

Built in governance

Pseudonymisation at source, federated authentication, role-based access, audit logging, GDPR accountability records in DAISY and HealthDCAT-AP dataset descriptions.

FAIR and AI-ready

Data are managed in levels (raw, quality-checked, aggregated) for FAIR reuse. This harmonised layer also enables machine-learning analyses and lets AI assistants explore metadata and aggregate statistics through a Model Context Protocol (MCP) interface, without access to record-level data.
